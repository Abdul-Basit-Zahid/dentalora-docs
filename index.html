    Dentalora documentation

    Dentalora is a WordPress theme for private dental clinics and boutique healthcare practices. It comes with a companion plugin, Dentalora Core, that adds 29 Elementor widgets, custom post types and the demo importer.

    Quick start in 3 steps

    1. Install the theme

      Unzip the download. Upload dentalora.zip and click Activate.

      Appearance → Themes → Add New → Upload Theme

    2. Activate the plugins

      Accept the notice that offers Dentalora Core, Elementor and One Click Demo Import.

      Begin installing plugins → Activate

    3. Import a demo

      Pick one of the six demos and click Import. Then replace the placeholder text and pictures.

      Appearance → Import Demo Data

    Total time on a normal host: about ten minutes. Details for every step are in the sections below.

    1. Before you start

    Requirements

    WordPress6.4 or newer
    PHP7.4 or newer (8.1 or newer recommended)
    ElementorThe free Elementor plugin from WordPress.org, needed for the page-builder widgets and the demo pages. Every widget, demo page, header and footer works with it. Elementor Pro is not required. The theme itself works without Elementor.

    With and without Elementor

    The theme on its own gives you a styled blog, the header and footer, the Customizer options, the presets and the dark mode. The demo homepages, the treatment, team and price pages and every section you see in the live demo are built from the widgets in Dentalora Core, and they need the free Elementor plugin. If you import a demo without Elementor, the pages are created but show nothing until you activate Elementor.

    What is in the download

    dentalora.zipThe theme. Upload this one in Appearance → Themes.
    dentalora-child.zipAn optional child theme for your own code changes.
    plugins/dentalora-core.zipThe companion plugin. The theme offers to install it for you; you can also upload it by hand.
    documentation/This guide: open index.html in any browser. It works offline, with its screenshots in the images folder.
    licensing.txt, changelog.txtLicences and credits for every font, icon and image, and the version history.
    Do not upload the whole download to WordPress. Unzip it first and upload the zip files inside.

    2. Installation

    1. In the WordPress admin go to Appearance → Themes → Add New → Upload Theme, choose dentalora.zip and click Install Now, then Activate.
    2. A notice asks you to install the plugins: Dentalora Core and Elementor (required for the demo layouts and widgets) and One Click Demo Import (recommended, for importing a demo). Click Begin installing plugins, select them all, choose Install, then Activate.
    3. If you prefer to install by hand, upload plugins/dentalora-core.zip in Plugins → Add New → Upload Plugin.

    To use the child theme, upload and activate dentalora-child.zip after the parent theme is installed (see section 13).

    3. Import the demo

    The demo gives you a finished site that you can then edit. Importing adds content; it does not delete your own content. The only things it removes are the unedited sample post "Hello world!" and sample page that WordPress creates on install. We still recommend using a new, empty site, or taking a backup first.

    1. Make sure Dentalora Core, Elementor and One Click Demo Import are active.
    2. Go to Appearance → Import Demo Data.
    3. Choose one of the six demos: Classic, Boutique, Precision, Clarity, Sunny or Aura.
    4. Click Import and confirm. Keep the page open until it says the import is complete. It can take a few minutes on slow hosting.

    What the import creates

    • Nineteen Elementor pages: six home pages (one per preset), About, Services, a service example, Our team, Pricing, Reviews, FAQ, Contact, Before and after, and four blog pages (Blog grid, Blog grid with sidebar, Blog list and Blog standard). Plus a Journal page (set as your posts page) and a Privacy policy placeholder.
    • Eleven journal posts with categories and topics (two of them in the sidebar and cover post layouts), six services, three doctors, six FAQ entries and six patient reviews.
    • Five menus, assigned to their locations (the main menu has a Journal dropdown with the blog pages), and blog sidebar widgets.
    • If your site still uses plain links (?p=123), they are switched to post names (Settings → Permalinks → Post name) so the demo menus and buttons open the right pages. A site with its own link structure keeps it.
    • The home page of the demo you chose is set as your front page and the matching preset is selected.
    • Clinic details (address, hours, phone, email) are filled in with fictional placeholders. Replace them under Dentalora → Clinic details.
    • Demonstration pictures in the media library: 41 placeholder pictures (soft gradients with a picture symbol, own artwork) and one map picture. No photograph is included: the photographs in the live demo are for preview purposes only and are not part of the purchase. Replace the placeholders with your own pictures. The before/after placeholders only differ in colour; they are not real treatments.
    The demo shows layout and structure. Every text, phone number, address, price and name in it is fictional. Replace all of it before you publish.

    Importing a second demo

    You can import another demo later. Pages and posts that already exist are imported again as copies, so delete the old demo pages first if you want a clean result.

    4. Set up without the demo

    1. Go to Dentalora → Clinic details and enter your address, opening hours, phone numbers and email, and, if you use an online booking service, its link. The top bar, footer, location widget and enquiry form use these.
    2. Create your pages. Edit a page with Elementor and use the Dentalora widgets from the panel (see section 10).
    3. Go to Settings → Reading, choose A static page and select your home page and your posts page.
    4. Create menus under Appearance → Menus and assign them to the locations listed in section 8.
    5. Choose a preset and your colours in Appearance → Customize.

    5. Customizer options

    Open Appearance → Customize. The Dentalora options are grouped in six sections.

    Style and coloursDesign preset, default colour scheme (light or dark), the light/dark toggle in the header, brand colour, accent colour, accent colour for small text, accent colour in dark mode and content width.
    HeaderShow the top bar, keep the header visible while scrolling, show the phone button, the Call and Book bar on phones, header button label and link, and whether the button opens in a new tab.
    Emergency bannerA short message above the header, with an optional link, two styles and a close button. See below.
    FooterCopyright text. Leave it empty to show the year and site title.
    BlogBlog layout (grid, list or standard), sidebar and its side, post layout (standard, with sidebar or full-width cover), table of contents on posts, reading progress bar, author box and related posts. See Blog pages and layouts.
    MotionFade-in and count-up on scroll, tilt on cards, parallax on large images and the button ripple. See section 7.

    Your site logo, site title and tagline are in the standard Site Identity section.

    Emergency banner

    Use it for a closure, holiday opening hours or an emergency line. Go to Appearance → Customize → Dentalora → Emergency banner, tick Show the banner above the header and write the message. Nothing is shown until you do both.

    • Link text and link address are optional. The address can be a page or tel: followed by a number. With no link of its own, the banner offers the emergency number from Clinic details, if there is one.
    • Style: deep red for alerts, or your brand colour for calmer notices.
    • Let visitors close the banner: a closed banner stays closed for the rest of that visit and returns on the next one, and straight away if you change the message.

    Online booking link

    If patients book through another website, put its address in Dentalora → Clinic details → Online booking link. The header button then points to it whenever its own link in the Customizer is empty. Links to another website always open in a new tab with a hidden hint for screen reader users; you can also tick Open the header button in a new tab for a link inside your own site. Buttons inside Elementor widgets have their own link setting, which can also be set to open in a new tab.

    6. Presets and dark mode

    All six presets share one design system. They differ in type, corner radius, colours, header and footer treatment and some section styling. Headlines use Manrope and text uses Figtree in every preset.

    ClassicExtra-bold, tightly set headlines and softly rounded corners. Deep navy with a clear red accent on cool white.
    BoutiqueBalanced headlines, rounded cards and a light footer. Near-black ink with an emerald accent on neutral white. This is the default.
    PrecisionLighter headlines, pill-shaped buttons, square cards, a wider layout and a dark footer. Deep petrol blue with a lime accent.
    ClarityA bright, friendly look: cool white surfaces, clinical blue with a soft sky accent, larger rounded corners, and a matching deep-navy dark mode. Choosing Clarity also changes the default colours shown in the Customizer colour pickers.
    SunnyMade for family and children's practices: white surfaces, blue with sunshine yellow, pill-shaped buttons and very rounded cards, with a matching navy dark mode. The Sunny home page has its own section order: parents' questions, a first-visit walkthrough and family payment plans.
    AuraMade for cosmetic and aligner practices: deep wine red with soft rose, light headlines and refined corners, with a matching dark mode. The Aura home page leads with a smile-design consultation, a before and after slider and priced treatment plans.

    Choose the preset for the whole site in the Customizer. To use a different preset on one page, edit the page and use the Dentalora design box in the editor sidebar (the box comes with the Dentalora Core plugin; the theme keeps the choice even if the plugin is switched off).

    Header image, background and block styles. Appearance > Customize > Header Image sets a picture behind the title of blog, archive, search and inner pages (darkened so the title stays readable). Colors > Background color and Background Image change the page background in the light colour scheme; dark mode keeps its own background. In the block editor, the Styles panel offers extra styles for core blocks: Card (Group), Rounded corners (Image), Soft (Button), Short accent line (Separator) and Check marks (List).

    Dark mode

    Set the default scheme in the Customizer. With the toggle enabled, visitors can switch between light and dark, and the colours fade smoothly between the two. Their choice is stored only in their own browser and is never sent to your server.

    Every Elementor widget has its own dark-mode colours in the Style tab under Colours (dark mode).

    7. Motion and animation

    Dentalora uses motion to make the page feel alive, never to slow it down. Every effect is optional and respects the visitor's device settings.

    Scroll revealHeadings glide up and card grids cascade in one after another (80 ms apart) as they scroll into view. Switch it off with Fade-in on scroll in the Customizer.
    Count-up numbersFigures in the Numbers band and in the hero statistics count up once when they appear. They follow the same Customizer switch as the scroll reveal. Screen readers always read the real value.
    Hover liftService, team, feature, info and journal cards, and blog cards, rise 5 px with a softer shadow; their pictures zoom a little and their arrows slide.
    Booking dotThe small dot on the "Next available" card pulses like a radar.
    FAQAnswers open with a smooth height change and a fade.
    Before and afterEach treatment panel fades in when you choose its tab.
    Page entranceThe header drops in and the page title rises in when a page loads.
    Light and darkColours fade when the visitor switches between light and dark.
    Tilt, parallax, rippleOptional extras, off by default: cards tilt toward the pointer, large pictures move slowly while you scroll and buttons show a ripple. Switch them on in the Customizer.
    Reduced motion is respected automatically. If a visitor's device is set to "reduce motion" (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce), none of these effects run: content is visible at once, numbers show their final value, the marquee strip stands still and nothing moves on hover. You do not have to do anything.

    Hover lift, the booking dot, the FAQ fade and the page entrance are plain CSS, so they follow only the visitor's reduced-motion setting. Scroll reveal and count-up also follow the Customizer switch.

    10. Elementor widgets

    Edit any page with Elementor. In the widget panel look for the Dentalora category. Widgets are designed to be stacked full width, one per section.

    Add a widget to a page, step by step

    1. Open Pages, hover the page and click Edit with Elementor (or create a page with Pages → Add New and click Edit with Elementor).
    2. Click the + icon on the page and choose a one-column structure. Widgets are made to fill the full width, so one widget per section works best.
    3. In the panel on the left, search for the widget or scroll to the Dentalora category and drag the widget into the section.
    4. Fill in the Content tab: the heading, the layout and the items. Most widgets start with example content so you can see the layout straight away.
    5. Open the Style tab to change colours for light and dark mode and to style each part (see the Style tab). Leave a field empty to use the design preset's value.
    6. Click Update. Check the page on a phone too: in the bottom bar of Elementor, the responsive mode button shows tablet and phone sizes.

    12. Appointment requests and the enquiry form

    Set up appointment requests in 7 steps

    1. Add your practice details. Go to Dentalora → Clinic details and fill in the address, phone, emergency number, email and opening hours. The header, top bar, footer, location widget and the form use them.
    2. Choose where requests go. In the same screen, under Enquiry form, set Send enquiries to and click Send a test email. If it does not arrive, see Making email reliable.
    3. Add the form to a page. Edit the page with Elementor and drag the Enquiry form widget into a section. In Layout, choose Appointment request for a compact form with reassurances and your phone number beside it, or Full enquiry form for a general contact form.
    4. Choose the questions. Switch on what you need: Ask for a preferred date (visitors pick a day in a calendar date picker), Ask for a preferred time of day (morning, afternoon or evening), Ask which treatment, Ask which dentist and Ask whether they are a new or existing patient.
    5. Fill the lists. Leave Treatment choices and Dentist choices empty to list your published Services and Doctors automatically, or type your own list, one per line.
    6. Check the wording. Write the Consent text, add your Privacy policy link and set the Button label. Every other fixed word, such as field labels and the note under the button, is in Labels and fixed texts.
    7. Answer the requests. New requests appear in Dentalora → Enquiries and in your email. Call or write back to agree the time, then set the status: New, Contacted, Booked or Closed.
    Requests, not automatic bookings. The form collects a preferred day and time; nothing is booked until your team confirms it. If patients should pick a free time slot themselves, use an online booking service and put its address in Dentalora → Clinic details → Online booking link: the header button then opens it (see section 5). You can link any Elementor button to it too.

    How the form works

    The Enquiry form widget works as an appointment request form. Visitors choose a reason, give their contact details, and can pick a preferred time of day, a preferred date and a treatment (your published services, or a list you type in the widget). The request arrives as an email to the address you enter in the widget or in Clinic details.

    The form has a hidden honeypot field, a security token that is fetched fresh when the page loads (so it also works behind page caching) and server-side validation. Every valid enquiry is emailed to you and, unless you switch it off, saved in a private inbox under Dentalora → Enquiries. A copy in the inbox means an enquiry is never lost when your host cannot send email.

    Two layouts

    In the widget, Layout offers the Full enquiry form and the Appointment request. The appointment request layout puts a short list of reassurances and your phone number beside a compact form: new or existing patient, treatment, dentist (your published team members, or a list you type), preferred date and time of day, name, phone, email and an optional note. Both layouts can ask for the dentist and the patient type; switch either off in the widget.

    The enquiry inbox

    • The list shows the name, phone and email (tap to call or write), what the visitor asked about, the preferred time, a status, whether the notification email went out, and when it arrived. The menu shows how many are new.
    • Open an enquiry to see everything that was sent, set its status (New, Contacted, Booked or Closed) and keep a private note. Use the bulk actions to mark several at once, and the filter above the list to see only new ones.
    • Only administrators can see the inbox. It has no public address and no REST route, and enquiries cannot be added by hand.

    Settings (Dentalora → Clinic details → Enquiry form)

    • Send enquiries to: the address that receives the emails. It defaults to the site administrator email.
    • Also save each enquiry on this site: switch it off to keep the form email-only.
    • Delete saved enquiries after: a number of days, checked once a day. 0 keeps them until you delete them. Choose a period that fits your local data protection rules.
    • Send a test email: sends a short message to the address above so you can see whether emails arrive.

    Making email reliable

    WordPress sends mail through your web host's basic mail function. Many hosts block it and many inboxes treat its messages as spam. If no SMTP or mail-service plugin is active, Dentalora shows a notice on its own screens with a link to find one (WP Mail SMTP and FluentSMTP are free choices), a test button and a Dismiss button. If an email later fails, the notice returns and the inbox marks that enquiry as Not delivered, so you know to look there.

    Saved enquiries are personal data. Mention the form in your privacy policy (the plugin adds suggested text under Settings → Privacy). Patients can ask for a copy of their data or for it to be erased: use Tools → Export Personal Data and Erase Personal Data, which include Dentalora enquiries found by email address.
    Do not ask patients to enter medical details in a contact form. Add a short privacy note under the form, and make sure your practice's privacy policy covers it.

    Focus point: keep the important part of a picture in view

    Cards, heroes and galleries crop pictures to fit their frame. By default the crop is centred, which can cut off a head at the top of a portrait. Tell Dentalora where the subject is:

    1. Go to Media → Library and click the picture.
    2. In Attachment details choose a value in the Focus point field.
    3. Click Update. The setting works everywhere the picture is used.
    Centre (default)No change.
    Upper middle (faces)Keeps the top quarter in view. Best for portraits and people.
    Upper left / Upper right (faces)Like upper middle, but leaning toward that side (useful when a floating card covers the other side).
    Top, Bottom, Left, RightKeeps that edge of the picture in view.
    Top left, Top right, Bottom left, Bottom rightKeeps that corner in view.

    17. Troubleshooting

    The widgets are not in Elementor

    Check that Dentalora Core and Elementor are both active. If Elementor is not active, imported demo pages stay empty, and a notice on the Dentalora screens and the demo import screen offers to install or activate it. You can dismiss the notice; it does not come back.

    What Dentalora Core changes in your site

    When you activate the plugin it registers its content types (services, doctors, reviews, before/after cases, FAQ and saved templates), refreshes the permalinks, and adds the service, doctor and template types to the list of content that Elementor may edit (Elementor → Settings → General). Deactivating it removes nothing. Deleting it removes nothing either, unless you ticked Remove data under Dentalora → Clinic details first; in that case your services, doctors, reviews, cases, FAQ entries, saved enquiries and the plugin settings are deleted. Pages and pictures created by the demo import are never deleted. The Elementor setting stays as it is.

    Treatment and team pages show "page not found"

    Go to Settings → Permalinks and click Save Changes.

    The demo import stops or times out

    Increase PHP max_execution_time to 300 and memory_limit to 256M, or ask your host to do so, then run the import again. In php.ini:

    max_execution_time = 300
memory_limit = 256M

    If you cannot edit php.ini, add this line to wp-config.php above the line that says That's all, stop editing:

    define( 'WP_MEMORY_LIMIT', '256M' );

    Enquiry emails do not arrive

    Install an SMTP plugin and send a test email from it. Check the spam folder.

    A page looks different after I changed the preset

    The preset can be overridden per page. Check the Dentalora design box in the page editor.

    Numbers do not count up, or sections do not fade in

    Check that Fade-in on scroll is on in the Customizer, and that your own device is not set to "reduce motion" (a visitor with that setting sees the final state at once, by design).

    18. Data and privacy

    • The theme and the Dentalora Core plugin do not send any data to other servers, and they load no files from other servers. Fonts, icons and pictures are in the download.
    • WordPress itself shows commenters' pictures from Gravatar (a service of Automattic). When a visitor opens a page with comments, their browser asks Gravatar for the picture, so Gravatar can see the visitor's IP address. You can turn avatars off under Settings → Discussion.
    • The enquiry form sends an email to the address you set and, unless you switch it off under Clinic details, saves the enquiry in a private inbox on your own site (name, phone, email, message, preferred time and treatment). Nothing is sent to another server. Only administrators can see saved enquiries, you can delete them after a number of days automatically, and the WordPress privacy tools can export or erase them by email address. Tell your visitors how you handle their messages in your privacy policy.
    • The map in the Location widget is a picture. It does not load a map from Google or anyone else. If you replace it with an embedded map, that service will receive your visitors' IP addresses.
    • If you pick a Google font in an Elementor typography control, Elementor loads it from Google's servers and Google can see your visitors' IP addresses. The fonts that come with Dentalora are loaded from your own server.
    • The light/dark choice is kept in the visitor's own browser storage and is not sent to your server.
